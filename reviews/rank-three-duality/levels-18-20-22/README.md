# The first supercharge exhausts the rank-three orthogonal excess at levels 18, 20 and 22

In the full relative local-operator complex of undeformed four-dimensional maximal Yang–Mills theory, let $j=2(E+J_L)$ be the conserved level. Count $SU(3)_R$ highest weights at fixed Lorentz projections as in the Fortuity census. The first differential $d_1$ pairs the following multiplets in $SO(7)$. In both groups the remaining classes at these three levels have representatives given by products of descendants of protected single traces.

| Level $j$ | Classical $SO(7)$ | Independent $d_1$ component pairs | Independent $d_1$ multiplet pairs | After $d_1$, in either group |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 18 | 985 | 1 | 1 | 983 |
| 20 | 2588 | 18 | 5 | 2578 |
| 22 | 6747 | 75 | 17 | 6713 |

Chang and Lin identified the first orthogonal–symplectic discrepancy. Choi and Lee computed the level-18 pairing. Kibe, van Leuven and Mathieson identified its monotone with the superconformal partner of the fourth glueball power. Fortuity computed the three classical censuses and identified the level-20 descendants by charges. The exact return matrices and the spanning minors below establish the descendant ranks and exhaust the complement. ## Descendant pairings

Write $S$ for the gaugino bilinear in the defining trace. Apply the six substitutions of a dotted index by a flavour index to $S^4$, in the divided-jet convention implemented in `jet_algebra.py`. The result has 23 trace monomials, dotted degrees $(1,1)$ and flavour degrees $(2,2,2)$. All linear lowerings and all five special conformal lowerings kill it. Applying the reverse word gives $1440 S^4$. This computation and the cited tree-cohomology identification give a nonzero level-18 target.

Use the first-order superconformal Ward identity on classical cohomology. Applied to the source of the level-18 pairing, each raising word therefore maps under $d_1$ to the same word on the monotone, up to the graded sign and the original nonzero coefficient. The words consist of six odd linear substitutions of level one, three odd translations of level two and two even translations of level three, ordered as recorded in `returned.json`. In each weight block apply the transposes in reverse order to the target words. The resulting matrix entries multiply the level-18 target. For relative levels zero, two and four all determinants are nonzero integers. The largest block has order seven and determinant $313456656384$. The second program acts directly on four trace factors. It verifies all $1+24+249$ entries, including the off-diagonal zeros. Thus the target words are independent in classical cohomology, and their first-order preimages are independent modulo the kernel of $d_1$.

The exact character test adds one copy of the source and target character for every independent pairing. Taking the $SU(3)$ Weyl numerator gives every classical orthogonal excess sector by sector, with $2,9,19$ differing sector–length pairs. `reopen.py` computes every sector difference from the six full input files. No restriction on dotted derivatives or on letter content is used in these censuses. ## Spanning by protected products

Fix the level, the number of letters and the total dotted degree. The remaining finite spaces are polynomial representations of the dotted $GL(2)$ and flavour $GL(3)$. Among the partitions of a fixed degree into at most two or three parts, respectively, the balanced partition is minimal in dominance order. Every irreducible constituent therefore contains that weight, by the semistandard tableau weight criterion. The same applies to any quotient by an invariant subspace. It suffices to saturate one balanced dotted and one balanced flavour weight at each grade. The exact Cartan-product span here is taken over the complete representations generated by the trace descendants.

The defining traces on the two three-dimensional Cartans agree on even products. Their three opposite eigenvalue pairs give a factor two per occupied trace and the extra zero eigenvalue of the orthogonal representation contributes zero. Every classical boundary restricts to zero since the tree variation is a bracket of jets. The rank of the restrictions is therefore a rational lower bound in both groups. Fortuity's exact cochain characters minus its two finite-field ranks give rational upper bounds on classical cohomology. At all 38 nonempty balanced weights the Cartan product lower bound equals the symplectic upper bound. At the largest weight, with dotted and flavour degrees $(1,1;3,3,2)$, the square submatrix selected at level 22 and length eight has order 2064. Its determinants modulo 1009 and 1013 are 628 and 923. The orthogonal upper bound there is 2072.

The orthogonal $d_1$ targets restrict to zero because the original $S^4$ vanishes on the three-colour Cartan and the jet operations commute with restriction. The product classes with independent restrictions have closed perturbative lifts. Their span meets the target span only in zero. Applying $d_1$ separates the paired sources from both spans. The sectorwise upper bounds now give equality for $SO(7)$ as well. The quantum product of Li, Liu and Chang supplies closed perturbative representatives for the entire complement, so all subsequent perturbative differentials have rank zero at these levels. If the protected single multiplets admit their physical representatives and their supersymmetric local products with these classical leading terms, the same spanning proof gives zero first and higher instanton differentials at these levels. This physical input is additional to the formal perturbative ring theorem. ## Reproduction

Use Python 3.10 or later with its standard library. The six census files decompress to their original pinned Git blobs. The first calculation uses the divided-jet commutators, the second acts on trace factors, and the third reconstructs all 38 minors and compares the full census characters. It rebuilds every selected polynomial by summing over set partitions of its factors among three labelled Cartan colours. It checks the resulting integer coefficients against the sequential colour multiplication and performs ordinary signed Gaussian elimination in each prime field.

~~~sh
shasum -a 256 -c SHA256SUMS
python3 -B verify.py | diff -u returned.json -
python3 -B trace_check.py | diff -u traces.json -
python3 -B reopen.py --out /tmp/so7-reopened.json
diff -u minors.json /tmp/so7-reopened.json
~~~

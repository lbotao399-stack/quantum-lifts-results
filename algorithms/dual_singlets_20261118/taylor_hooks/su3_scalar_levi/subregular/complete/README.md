# The full rank-one chart at SU(3) levels 32 and 34

For each published BMN source $\mathcal O_0$, the complete first image $Q_1\mathcal O_0$ is now expanded in the rank-one scalar chart. The positions $23$ and $31$ in its odd matrix are the fields $f$ and $g$. Write $(f,g)$ for the ideal generated by all their Taylor coefficients. The signed classical equations $Q_0f=3af$ and $Q_0g=-3ag$ preserve this ideal and its square. Here $a$ is the $11$ entry of the same matrix. | 32 | 331201 | 104825 | 253788 | 52153 | 32343 | 0 |
| 34 | 517468 | 229290 | 880876 | 166368 | 94148 | 0 |

Each input word contains seven coefficients of the even matrix and four of the odd matrix. Their polynomial degrees on the chart are at most one and two. Hence $7+2\cdot4=15$ bounds the full expansion. The nonzero counts by polynomial degree are

Moving a longest trace to the front of a trace product gives its graded permutation sign. Grouping all words with the same extracted trace allows the other factors to be added before matrix multiplication. The native expansion uses checked signed integer additions and multiplications. The original through-degree-nine expansions agree coefficientwise at both levels. At level 34 the independent A8 expansion also agrees in every coefficient of degrees ten and eleven. The odd field $d$ is the $21$ matrix entry. Its equation $Q_0d=fg$ makes each $d$ coefficient a closed spectator modulo $(f,g)^2$. For each monomial in these spectators we solved $Q_0\mathcal O_1=-Q_1\mathcal O_0$, retaining both the constant and linear terms in $f,g$ in the same system. The replay multiplies the assembled correction by the classical differential on the original chart coordinates and clears the common denominator, 3 at level 32 and 120 at level 34. This proves the first equation in the quotient. The global invariant first correction and the higher supercharge obstructions remain open. ## A common correction for the two filtrations

Count the Taylor coefficients of the odd field $d$ in each matrix monomial. Only $Q_0d=fg$ lowers this count. Each such term introduces two coefficients of $f,g$. All other variations preserve the number of $d$ coefficients and preserve or increase the total $f,g$ degree. In particular, modulo $(f,g)^2$ every monomial in the $d$ coefficients is a separate spectator.

First solve in descending $d$ count with the full $f,g$ dependence. Leave all coefficients of this correction intact. From the previously verified correction modulo $(f,g)^2$, add the components with $d$ count below the lowest count already solved. The added terms have no effect on the solved high counts. At $f,g$ degree zero and one, the higher and lower components solve independent equations. The resulting single rational correction has the following integer replay in the untruncated chart.

| Level | Lowest solved $d$ count | Terms in high-count correction | Terms in combined correction | Common multiplier | Support of the surviving residual | Terms |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|
| 32 | 1 | 87340 | 93362 | 120 | zero $d$ count, $f,g$ degree 2 through 4 | 153527 |
| 34 | 1 | 234227 | 252847 | 11520 | zero $d$ count, $f,g$ degree 2 through 4 | 499085 |

The three surviving counts are 40184, 67248 and 46095 at level 32, and 134238, 217809 and 147038 at level 34. These residuals belong to the particular corrections displayed here. A nonzero obstruction would require testing their images in the full quotient by correction freedom. ## The complete first equation at level 32

The descending equations at counts three, two, one and zero of the odd entry $d$ require 378, 13792, 73170 and 121300 correction terms, respectively. Multiplication on all 124 Taylor generators gives the polynomial identity on the chart

$$
190080\bigl(Q_1\mathcal O_0+Q_0\mathcal O_1\bigr)=0 .
$$

The complete first image has 253788 matrix monomials and the rational primitive has $378+13792+73170+121300=208640$ terms. This replay uses off-diagonal ghost degrees zero through four and all polynomial degrees of the original 331201 trace terms. [Rational primitive](j32_full_first_primitive.json.gz) · [Integer replay](j32_full_first.json)

The last system has 644395 equations, 417018 trial columns and modular rank 309245. The last 121300 coefficients were independently recovered from the two moduli 2147483647 and 1000000007. In the integer right-hand-side units of the native solve their common denominator is 4752. For each trial denominator the algorithm takes the centred residues of the scaled coordinates. Exact multiplication supplies rows that can discard subsequent denominators. Three full integer multiplications sufficed. Restoring the input unit $1/40$ gives $40\cdot4752=190080$. ### Support in scalar space

On the open set where the first scalar has invertible entry $B^2{}_1(0)$, the moving frame and its inverse are regular. The four positive-order frame series and their $Q_0$ variations are coordinate pairs. Contracting each pair by its degree-counting homotopy identifies the cohomology with that of the reduced eight-field chart tensored with functions of the constant frame. On the projection to these remaining variables the original matrix series is conjugated by a constant frame, which cancels in each trace. Thus the polynomial identity gives a primitive in the localized original matrix complex. [Frame, inverse and differential](../README.md#the-chart-at-a-nonzero-rank-one-scalar)

Every infinitesimal flavour transformation commutes with $Q_0$ and extends to this localization by the quotient rule, even when it changes the inverted entry. Applying it to the primitive gives a primitive for the transformed first image on the same open set. The fixed-level polynomial representation is finite-dimensional. Its Lie algebra generates the linear span of the connected flavour-group orbit. Consequently every finite flavour translate of the first image is exact on this open set. Conjugation by a constant colour matrix preserves all trace expressions.

If any scalar in a triple is nonzero, a flavour transformation makes it the first. A nonzero traceless matrix $B$ has a vector $v$ for which $v,Bv$ are independent. Otherwise every line is invariant and $B$ is scalar, hence zero. Choosing a colour frame beginning with $v,Bv$ gives $B^2{}_1(0)=1$. Transporting the localized primitive shows that the possible support of this particular first obstruction in the 24-dimensional space of scalar triples is contained in the origin. A global invariant polynomial primitive still requires a calculation there.

## The complete first equation at level 34

The first image of the charge-conjugation-even level-34 operator changes sign when the two spacetime derivatives are exchanged. Exchanging the Taylor labels and reordering the odd factors verifies this identity on all 880876 coefficients. The exchange commutes with $Q_0$ on all 124 chart generators. Each two-element orbit therefore needs one coefficient. A fixed monomial survives precisely when reordering its odd factors supplies a minus sign. The projection gives 449696 independent orbit coordinates in the first image. [Exchange check](j34_spin_preparation.json)

After the positive-$d$ equations are solved, the last system has 615674 equations and 345235 trial coefficients. At the prime 2147483647 its rank is 255247. Keeping the 145059 nonzero coefficients gives a matrix with 440837 rows of full column rank at five further primes. The other 174837 rows have zero right-hand side and no entry in these columns. Lifting these coefficients by the Chinese remainder theorem and undoing the exchange projection gives one rational polynomial with 514322 monomials. Multiplication in the original chart gives

$$
69120\bigl(Q_1\mathcal O_0+Q_0\mathcal O_1\bigr)=0 .
$$

All 880876 input coefficients and every additional monomial produced by $Q_0\mathcal O_1$ were included. The support argument above now applies at level 34 as well. At both levels, the only remaining scalar triple at which the first obstruction can be supported is the origin. A global polynomial primitive is needed before the second obstruction of the level-34 operator can be evaluated. [Rational polynomial](j34_full_first_primitive.json.gz) · [Integer identity](j34_full_first.json) · [Six modular systems](j34_spin_reconstruction.json)

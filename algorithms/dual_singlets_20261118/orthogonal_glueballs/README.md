## A source in finite colours

For a proposed order $r$ into $S^p$, the first-jet relation has $p-r$ ghosts of each dotted component, $r$ scalars of each flavour, and length $2p+r$. The source has one fewer letter. The four rows above have $(p,r)=(5,1),(6,1),(9,3),(10,3)$ respectively and defining dimension $p+2r+2$. By the orthogonal second fundamental theorem every finite-colour relation is a linear combination of determinants antisymmetrizing $p+2r+3$ vector slots on each side, with remaining slots paired. [Orthogonal second fundamental theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/1102.3221)

Use the two auxiliary Grassmann vectors for the left and right sets of slots in the determinant. Open paths have endpoint types left-left, left-right and right-right. For each dotted component at most one identical odd single-letter path of each type survives. There are $p-r-3$ metric edges outside the determinant. The number of odd letters is

$$
2(p-r)=6+2(p-r-3).
$$

Thus all six odd single-letter paths occur. Each remaining metric edge joins two further ghosts in one double path. All scalars are single paths. The scalar single paths transform as the three-dimensional symmetric square of the auxiliary two-space. Their flavour singlet is the $r$th power of the determinant of their three auxiliary components against their three flavours. The six odd single paths have one top exterior product.

The double-ghost path is symmetric under simultaneous exchange of the auxiliary and dotted endpoints, by skew transposition and the odd interchange. It splits into a scalar and a tensor symmetric in each pair, of dimension $1+9$. A predecessor relation with one second jet must use a second ghost jet on a single path. Either of the other second jets would require more first ghosts than the determinant slots can accommodate. The three dotted components of that jet map under $Q_0$ to the symmetric products $\lambda_+^2$, $\lambda_+\lambda_-+\lambda_-\lambda_+$ and $\lambda_-^2$. This supplies the entire nine-dimensional double-path tensor. If a homogeneous invariant contains $k>0$ tensor factors, polarizing one factor into the second jet maps back to $k$ times that invariant. Consequently only the pure power of the scalar double path can survive in the relation quotient.

The full spin/flavour character calculation gives three terminal relations and two predecessor relations for $SO(17)$, and five and four for $SO(18)$. For three double paths the invariant monomials have tensor degrees $0,2,3$. For four they have degrees $0,2,3,4,4$. They are generated by the scalar, the trace and second elementary coefficient of the symmetric tensor square, and the determinant of the tensor. This follows by restricting the $3$-by-$3$ tensor to diagonal matrices with distinct squared singular values. Those matrices are dense up to the two complex rotation groups, and the two symmetric coefficients and the signed determinant generate their polynomial invariants. The listed counts attain the independently computed character multiplicities. The quotient has dimension one in both groups. The terminal relation has odd total length. Each multitrace contains an odd-length trace. Reversing that trace by adjacent graded interchanges gives its negative by orthogonal transposition. Each difference in an interchange is a tree variation of a second jet. Dividing the telescoping identity by two constructs a primitive term by term. Its tree image is the determinant relation, so its specialization is a classical cocycle. In the exact sequence from stable traces to finite traces its connecting class is the surviving relation. This gives one source modulo stable cycles in each of the two third-order source blocks. [Tree primitive](../orthogonal_odd_trace_primitive.py)

## Computing the first obstruction

The determinant coefficient can be computed without expanding the permutations of the colour slots. In the auxiliary two-space take $J$ with rows $(0,1),(-1,0)$ and the ordered basis $H,E,F$ of traceless matrices, with $H$ diagonal $(1,-1)$, $E$ upper and $F$ lower elementary. In the Pfaffian quotient $\operatorname{Pf}(J(1+A))/\operatorname{Pf}(J)$ the single-letter part of $A$ couples each scalar and ghost to its own traceless auxiliary matrix. The nine scalar markers are commuting. The six ghost markers are odd and precede their colour matrices, ordered first by $+,-$ and then by $H,E,F$. The double path couples the auxiliary identity to the colour matrix $\lambda_+\lambda_- -\lambda_-\lambda_+$ with one commuting marker. Here $J$ acts with the colour identity.

Apply the $r$th power of the determinant of the scalar-marker derivatives, take all six ordered odd coefficients, and extract double-marker degree $p-r-3$. This picks total Pfaffian degree $p+2r+3$. The Pfaffian of a skew matrix of size twice the defining dimension has smaller degree, so this coefficient vanishes in the stated physical group. The identity $\log(\operatorname{Pf}(J(1+A))/\operatorname{Pf}(J))=\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}\log(1+A)$ gives the signed stable trace expansion. The odd-trace primitive of this particular coefficient fixes the source used in the table. [Rational coefficient extraction](../orthogonal_glueball_first_pair.py)

Only two trace patterns enter the chosen product of bilinears. The two letters on which $Q_1$ acts must be a two-flavour jet and its complementary scalar. Acting within the primitive of one three-letter trace yields one target trace. Acting within the primitive of one five-letter trace can split it into two target traces. Every spectator is already a bilinear. Joining distinct traces would require more input traces than the total source length permits. In the canonical trace order $\operatorname{Tr}(\lambda_-\lambda_+)=-S$ the contributions are

In each evaluated target there are zero finite-colour first-jet relations in the full spin/flavour singlet. The source is a singlet. In the stable quotient each even trace is graded symmetric, and products of these traces give independent coordinates. Extraction of the displayed coordinate therefore detects a nonzero finite-colour class. At $SO(17)$ and $SO(18)$ the complete intermediate singlet has dimension $10$, Cartan rank $3$, and Cartan kernel dimension $7$. The two-dimensional kernel obtained by projecting to a single Taylor diagram is a smaller space. The first-differential certificate uses the full physical singlet. The candidate finite-rank source for the third differential thus dies on the first page in both groups.

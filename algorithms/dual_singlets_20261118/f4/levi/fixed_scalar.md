# The two exceptional sources at a fixed scalar

Every closed $F_4$ BMN source of flavour weight $(m,m,m)$ and length $3m-1$, for $m=4,5$, is a classical boundary after fixing any one scalar to any complex value. These are the sources at levels 24 and 30 for the second and third supercharge tests. The primitive is in the component algebra at the fixed value. A single gauge-invariant polynomial primitive in all three variable scalars remains open. [Previous calculation for a nonzero semisimple part](README.md#fixing-a-scalar) · [All nilpotent fibres](nilpotent_fibres.json)

## The differential on the fibre

Fix $\phi^1=B$, and write $Y=\phi^2$, $Z=\phi^3$. With the three matter fermions $\psi_i$ and the field strength $f$ in the archived BMN conventions, the scalars have zero variation and
$$
Q_0\psi_1=[Y,Z],\qquad Q_0\psi_2=[Z,B],\qquad Q_0\psi_3=[B,Y],\qquad
Q_0 f=[B,\psi_1]+[Y,\psi_2]+[Z,\psi_3].
$$
Put $\mathfrak z=\ker\operatorname{ad}_B$, the centralizer of $B$ in the complex Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$. The invariant trace form identifies $\mathfrak g/\operatorname{im}\operatorname{ad}_B$ with $\mathfrak z^*$. [Signed letter equations](../../scalar_neighbors.py)

Choose complements to the kernel and image of $\operatorname{ad}_B$. Each transverse entry of $Y,Z$ is the variation of one entry of $\psi_3,\psi_2$ with its indicated sign. Quotient by these pairs. To check the quotient on cohomology, give $f$ filtration degree two, $\psi_1$ degree one and the remaining letters degree zero. On the associated graded the differential acts only on the pairs. Each odd variable maps to its independent even partner, so their polynomial complex has cohomology the constants. The filtration is bounded in each fixed degree of the last two flavours.

After the quotient, $Y,Z$ take values in $\mathfrak z$ and $\psi_2,\psi_3$ take values in $\mathfrak z^*$. The part of $Q_0 f$ in $\operatorname{im}\operatorname{ad}_B$ is an invertible linear map of the transverse part of $\psi_1$, translated by the projection of $[Y,\psi_2]+[Z,\psi_3]$. This odd coordinate is closed since $[Y,Z]\in\mathfrak z$. Pair it with the corresponding part of $f$ and quotient again. The surviving $\psi_1$ takes values in $\mathfrak z$, and its differential is $[Y,Z]$. The surviving $f$ takes values in $\mathfrak z^*$, and its differential is the sum of the two coadjoint actions induced by $Y,Z$. Both coadjoint fermions are closed. In particular any coefficient in the ideal generated by the components of $[Y,Z]$, multiplied by a coadjoint fermion, is a boundary.

## Every nilpotent orbit

For $B\ne0$ nilpotent, take an $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ triple with raising element $B$ and grading element $h$, so $[h,B]=2B$. The connected centralizer of the triple is reductive. Choose the two complements in its representations using the highest and lowest weight lines of the triple. If $r$ is the highest weight of a summand of the adjoint representation, its centralizer line has height $r\ge0$, and the dual line has height $-r$.

Combine conjugation by $t^h$ with the flavour rescaling $\phi^1\mapsto t^{-2}\phi^1$. This fixes $B$. The rectangular flavour representation is the $m$th power of the determinant, so a restricted source scales by $t^{-2m}$. The source is linear in a fermion. An entry of $\psi_1$ has nonnegative height and carries neither the first flavour nor a negative power of $t$. It contributes zero. An entry of $\psi_2$ or $\psi_3$ on a dual line of height $-r$ carries one first flavour. The total height of the remaining $2m-1$ scalars must equal $r-2(m-1)$.

The last two flavours act on these two fermions as a doublet. To give the determinant to power $m$, the scalar tensor must have two-row Young diagram $(m,m-1)$. Its character is the product of the symmetric-power characters in degrees $m,m-1$ minus their product in degrees $m+1,m-2$. The calculation resolves this character by height and by the torus commuting with the triple, then takes invariants of the connected reductive centralizer using the Weyl denominator.

For each representative in de Graaf's Table 3, take the torus annihilated by its listed roots from Table 8. Their pairwise differences are absent from the root system and their coroots span $h$. These checks give a triple centralized by the torus. At each torus weight the difference of the height-$r$ and height-$(r+2)$ adjoint multiplicities gives the centralizer multiplicity at height $r$. At height zero its zero-weight multiplicity equals the dimension of this torus in every case, so it is maximal in the reductive centralizer. | Weighted Dynkin diagram | Orbit dimension | Invariant coefficients at level 24 | At level 30 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| $2200$ | 42 | 1 | 1 |
| $1012$ | 42 | 2 | 1 |
| Other 13 nonzero orbits | 16 to 48 | 0 each | 0 each |

On both dimension-42 orbits the height-zero centralizer is $\mathfrak{sl}_2$, the least positive height is two and the largest height is ten. The height-zero part of $[Y,Z]$ is $[Y_0,Z_0]$, where $Y_0,Z_0$ are the height-zero scalar entries. The bracket identifies $\wedge^2\mathfrak{sl}_2$ with $\mathfrak{sl}_2$. Its three entries generate the ideal of the three two-by-two minors of the matrix with columns $Y_0,Z_0$. Modulo that ideal the two columns have rank at most one. In every degree the remaining zero-height factors have totally symmetric flavour indices, since each pair can be interchanged by one of the minor relations. Each of the $m-1$ two-box columns of $(m,m-1)$ must therefore contain a positive-height scalar. The required and available heights obey
$$
m=4:\quad 2(m-1)=6>10-2(m-1)=4,\qquad
m=5:\quad 8>2 .
$$
All five remaining coefficients are therefore in the commutator ideal and give boundaries. At $B=0$ every source term contains at least $m-1$ first scalars and vanishes directly. Together with the earlier calculation for a scalar with nonzero semisimple part, this covers every value of $B$. The rectangular flavour symmetry allows any nonzero linear combination to be chosen as the first scalar.

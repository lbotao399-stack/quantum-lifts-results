# Product sources for the seventh glueball

In the relative holomorphic jet complex of undeformed $F_4$ theory, $S$ denotes the glueball. On $E_2=H(H(Q_0),d_1)$ both $[S^6]$ and $[S^7]$ are nonzero. A product of positive-degree classical classes, or a linear combination of their projections to the level-42 source, can hit $[S^7]$ under $d_2$ **if and only if** an incoming $d_2$ hits $[S^6]$. This result covers products whose individual factors have yet to be tested against $d_1$. ## Which classical products reach the second page

The selected Taylor diagram is $(5,5,3,3)$. Its 16 Taylor slots are distributed over 15 letters. In any homogeneous classical product, exactly one factor has one more slot than letters. All other factors have only first jets and may be grouped together. Every positive-order bare supercharge vanishes on a first-jet factor by flavour counting. Write $m$ for the number of slots in this grouped factor. [Taylor selection and shear identities](../first_page/README.md#the-fifth-glueball-survives-to-perturbative-infinity)

* If $m\ge8$, the other factor has at most eight slots. A nonzero $d_1$ would require a Taylor diagram containing a three-by-three square.
* If $m=7$, the only possible diagram for the other factor is $(3,3,3)$, with first target $(4,4)$. Its pure-gaugino classical target is spanned by $[S^4]$. Hitting $[S^4]$ would hit $[S^7]$ after multiplication by $[S^3]$ on the classical page.
* Through five slots every positive-degree terminal class is a product of the invariant bilinear. At six slots the terminal tensors are the fifteen products of three bilinears and one symmetric sextic colour tensor, as verified below. The sextic has Taylor diagram $(1,1,1,1,1,1)$ because the first-jet construction transposes the colour symmetry. Its six rows cannot fit in $(5,5,3,3)$. [Labelled colour calculation through degree five](https://github.com/lbotao399-stack/quantum-lifts-results/blob/78d170a26afc2a16cae43f123df86d5880be6196/reviews/independent-f4/sixth-glueball-classical-products/README.md)

For each remaining product, leave one bilinear as a spectator and associate the other factors first. Its Taylor diagram $(1,1)$ removes one box from each of two distinct rows. The vertical-strip Pieri rule and the first-differential weight shift give the following exhaustive list.

| Remaining diagram | First target | Terminal cochain dimension |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| $(5,5,2,2)$ | No allowed weight | |
| $(5,4,3,2)$ | $(6,5,2)$ | 238 |
| $(4,4,3,3)$ | $(5,5,3)$ | 209 |

Both target components occur in the level-36 intermediate singlet at $(5,5\mid1,1,1)$. The four terminal multiplicities there are $36+150+238+209=633$. The classical map onto this singlet has rank 633. Indeed, the homogeneous annihilator has at most 40 coordinates, and a 40-row minor gives $194027\bmod1000003$ and $30888\bmod1000033$. Each target component thus has zero classical cohomology. Taylor projection commutes with the classical map and with the shifted first differential on cohomology. Every product under consideration is a sum of projected products of $d_1$-cycles. [Native intermediate rank](../first_page/README.md#one-row-decides-the-second-differential)

## Native six-colour calculation

The character of six labelled adjoints gives 80 invariant tensors. Insert a Lie bracket in a five-colour invariant for each of the fifteen possible pairs of inputs, using the sixteen five-colour tensors from the cited calculation. Every one of the resulting 240 columns has a classical primitive given by replacing the bracket with its second jet. Adjoin all fifteen metric products and the full polarization of $\operatorname{Tr}_{26}(X^6)$. The metric is $B=\operatorname{Tr}_{26}/6$.

| Same ordered minor over both fields | Modulo 1009 | Modulo 1013 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 64 bracket columns and 16 terminal candidates on 80 native points | 86 | 1000 |
| 16 terminal candidates on 16 Cartan points | 413 | 278 |

The first minor exhausts the character dimension over the rationals. Every classical bracket vanishes on the Cartan, where the second minor proves independence of all sixteen remaining classes. Hence the labelled six-colour terminal quotient has dimension $80-64=16$. Its only class modulo metric products is represented by the symmetric sextic. ## Image at the pure target

The Leibniz rule on $E_2$ acts on the factor with one extra slot. The unchanged positive-degree factor of any term contributing to a pure first-gaugino target must itself have only first gauginos. Its invariant classical quotient is generated by $S$. On the target weight that factor is $[S^k]$, $k\ge1$. For $k\ge2$, hitting $[S^{7-k}]$ would hit $[S^5]$ after multiplication by $[S^{k-2}]$. The fifth power survives to perturbative infinity. The unit in the case $k=7$ is also permanent. Only $k=1$ can contribute. Conversely if $d_2[X]=[S^6]$, the closed glueball gives

$$
d_2([X][S])=[S^7]\ne0\quad\text{on }E_2.
$$

Thus an incoming $d_2$ on the seventh power independent of the sixth must be sought outside the span of positive-degree classical products. The full incoming maps for the two powers remain undetermined.

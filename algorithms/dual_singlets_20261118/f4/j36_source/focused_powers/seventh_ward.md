# A necessary mixed-jet cancellation for a second differential into the seventh F₄ glueball

In the full relative holomorphic jet complex, a source hitting the glueball power $[S^7]$ at $j=42$ under $d_2$ must contain **both** a matter-fermion term and a differentiated-scalar term. The statement applies to every representative of a contributing leading classical cycle at the target spin and flavour, after projection to the singlets. The rank of the full incoming map is open. The [global sixth-power calculation](sixth_survival.md) excludes the product of a sixth-power source with $S$.

## The scalar and pure-ghost equations have a simultaneous solution

Write $Q_0,Q_1,Q_2$ for the classical supercharge and its first two corrections. The source has dotted degrees $(5,5)$, scalar flavour degrees $(2,2,2)$ and fifteen letters. It has exactly one second jet. Its possibilities are the matter fermion, a differentiated scalar, and a second ghost jet. $Q_1$ needs three scalar flavour slots across its two inputs. Only the matter fermion together with a complementary scalar supplies them. The bare $Q_2$ vanishes. Every classical cycle admits a first correction since the intermediate classical image has rank 2959 in dimension 2959. For the target covector $\lambda$ and its first Ward solution $\eta$, one has

$$
\lambda Q_1=\eta Q_0,\qquad
\lambda(S^7)=-194893931232000,\qquad
\lambda(d_2[X])=-\eta Q_1X\quad(Q_0X=0).
$$

Quotient the exterior algebra of the two first dotted ghosts by the ideal generated by their three classical brackets, the two equal-species brackets and the mixed bracket. The first Ward equation implies that $\eta$ annihilates this ideal in its degree. Every generator times a first-jet monomial is the $Q_0$ image of a second ghost jet times that monomial, and $Q_1$ on this predecessor vanishes by flavour degree. On a matter-fermion cochain $Q_1$ leaves every existing dotted spectator in the exterior product. Consequently $\eta Q_1$ still vanishes when its input spectator lies in the ghost bracket ideal.

In this quotient the matter-fermion split is the identity on the dotted spectators tensored with the scalar bracket map. Its kernel is the dotted quotient tensored with the scalar kernel. The full six-scalar colour calculation at flavour $(2,2,2)$ proves that this kernel in the scalar variables is spanned by classical boundaries and multiples of the closed marked bilinear, with arbitrary spectator colours. The result holds before gauge averaging. Boundaries pair to zero. On averaging each marked-bilinear product, the projection to gauge invariants can be taken on the remaining first jets. The resulting product has zero second image at $S^7$ by the level-42 product calculation. Thus $\eta Q_1$ annihilates the entire kernel of the scalar bracket map tensored with the dotted quotient. Exactness of invariant projection over the rationals permits this test on the gauge and flavour singlets. It follows by linear algebra that there is a scalar covector on the image of the bracket map in this quotient sending $Q_0X$ to $\eta Q_1X$ for every matter-fermion input. Extend it linearly to the whole terminal space and average over the compact gauge, spin and flavour groups. Pulling it back from the ghost quotient gives a rational solution of the scalar and all pure-ghost equations simultaneously. Equivalently one can project to rational invariant summands by reductivity. Any cycle comprising only matter-fermion and second-ghost terms has zero second pairing. A cycle with only differentiated scalars and second ghosts has $Q_1X=0$. This proves the necessary cancellation.

The remaining equation requires this extension to vanish on every differentiated-scalar split, the mixed brackets of a first dotted ghost and a scalar. Equivalently the already prescribed functional on the scalar bracket image must vanish on its intersection with the differentiated-scalar image, both taken modulo pure-ghost brackets. Spin and flavour equivariance identify the two dotted choices and the three scalar choices. Consistency on the complete colour space of one such choice decides the full incoming $d_2$. A nonzero value on the intersection would give a lift.

For a fixed dotted derivative the other five scalar colours have Young shape $(2,2,1)$, obtained by removing the marked scalar from the flavour determinant square. Adjoining the distinguished scalar colour decomposes by the one-box Pieri rule into $(3,2,1)$, $(2,2,2)$ and $(2,2,1,1)$, each once. In the middle summand, marking one of the six scalar positions and summing over its positions is the scalar part of a total derivative. Erasing the marking composes with this operation to give six times the identity. Its split is the simultaneous adjoint action of the first dotted ghost on all six scalars. Gauge invariance moves this action onto the ghost spectators, giving pure-ghost brackets, already annihilated. The independent differentiated-scalar test therefore needs only the components $(3,2,1)$ and $(2,2,1,1)$.

There is a second exact reduction on these rows. If the five undifferentiated scalar spectators contain a scalar bracket, replace that bracket by its matter-fermion primitive. The classical derivative of the resulting two-mark cochain is the original differentiated-scalar cochain plus a matter-fermion cochain, with the relative Koszul sign. Their sum is a classical boundary, so its second pairing vanishes. The prescribed extension agrees with $\eta Q_1$ on the matter-fermion term, and $Q_1$ vanishes on the differentiated-scalar term. Applying $Q_0^2=0$ shows that the extension already annihilates the split of the original differentiated-scalar cochain. The remaining tests can therefore be taken modulo scalar brackets among the five spectators.

## Counting the homogeneous scalar equations

The six-scalar flavour singlet has colour representation of Young shape $(2,2,2)$ in the 52-dimensional adjoint. The hook formula gives dimension 129428650. In every nonzero dominant weight the recorded scalar split rank and checked kernel dimension sum to the full source dimension. The total split rank over nonzero weights is 40848456. To find the zero-weight rank, use the Weyl denominator on this image character. Its invariant multiplicity is three, fixed by the native three-by-three scalar minor with determinant 292863838464. In the Weyl formula for the trivial multiplicity the identity term is precisely the missing zero-weight rank, with unit coefficient. It gives 149747. $$
129428650-40848456-149747=88430447 .

## What the SO13 j42/j48 source actually contains

The Dot archive's `seed_promote.py` and `seed_promoted_cycle.py` use fifteen scalar first jets of weight `(5,5,5)` and one gaugino of each dotted sign: seventeen first jets in total. Each relation is a size14 Gram determinant with three remaining ordinary edges. The two gaugino vertices are incorporated into the determinant endpoints. The normalized reversal primitive has length16 and pre-return grading `(5,5,5;1,1)`. Six strict odd Taylor operations give the published `(3,3,3;4,4)_16` source at j42. Its third pairing is `-921600 S^7`. The j48 chain is explicitly this completed chain multiplied by S.

These seeds are not literal scalar-primitive-times-S expressions. They nevertheless have that product's grading; a cohomology identification would require a separate quotient calculation. It is unnecessary for the present exclusion because the independent BMN product certificate below is decisive.

The SO14 j48 third differential is reported in discussion entry D014. The inspected Dot archive packages only SO11/SO12/SO13. The available Codex SO14 archive proves the earlier-page target certificate, not a scalar-parent identification. No additional scalar lineage is inferred from that reported SO14 result.

## Why both SO13 j30 scalar parents were already established

The existing `bmn13/README.md` proves a complete relation quotient at weight `(7,5,5)`, length17:

`dim I16 = 69`, `dim I17 = 42`, `rank(Q0:I16→I17) = 40`.

Its two complementary rows are explicitly scalar bilinears multiplying degree15 all-singleton Pfaffian relations. Their stable reversal primitives represent two independent classes modulo stable cycles. In particular their parent classes cannot be zero modulo stable cycles.

The parent types are fixed by the auxiliary representation. Three traceless 2×2 auxiliary matrices transform as three vectors for SO3. Their invariant polynomial algebra is generated by Gram quadratics and the oriented determinant; its GL3 decomposition is multiplicity-free with all row lengths even or all odd. At odd degree15, only the all-odd types occur. Removing a horizontal two-box strip from `(7,5,5)` allows `(5,5,5)`, `(6,5,4)`, `(7,5,3)`; the middle type is excluded by the auxiliary invariant condition. Each remaining parent type occurs once. Therefore two independent products force both `(5,5,5)` and `(7,5,3)` parent directions to be nonzero.

This is an implicit existing existence proof. Choosing new explicit highest-weight representatives or new rational duals does not create new classes.

## SO15 `(7,5,5)`: two completeness gates and a smaller-rank test

### Gate 1: the old quotient image has exact rational rank40

The prior certificate provides 29 independent Q0-closed relations in the 69-dimensional I16. Its displayed constructions are closed by Q0², infinitesimal conjugation, or a closed trace multiplier. Thus the rational image rank is at most `69−29=40`. Its 40 independent actual Q0-image rows attain that bound. The new calculation reuses this archived character/closed-row completeness argument; it does not replace it with a modular rank lower bound.

### Gate 2: the tested all-singleton row is the proposed highest-weight family

The old unmodified probe code uses five epsilon triples and two flavour-zero slots, selecting precisely `(7,5,5)`. The all-singleton auxiliary highest-weight space is one-dimensional, with basis polynomial `D^5 qAA` in the scalar lane's notation. This follows by taking the even `(6,4,4)` Gram highest-weight component and multiplying by the oriented determinant. No new Gram normalization is imposed by this argument.

To obtain a degree17 all-singleton row, the program is invoked with its path-cost argument set to16, giving zero non-singleton cost at total degree17. This argument is used only to select the all-singleton recipe. The image bank being tested remains SO13. For seed0 the auxiliary vectors in the code's `(a,b,c)` coordinates are `(1,-1,-3)`, `(-4,-4,0)`, `(1,-3,-4)`; their determinant is −16 and `Tr(M_A²)/2=4`, so the selected component is nonzero.

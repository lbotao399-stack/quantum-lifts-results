# Cartan restriction of higher differentials in the formal `N=4` BPS complex

**Statement.** Use the perturbative holomorphic Taylor-jet complex of Li–Liu–Chang, normalized as Q(u)=Q0+u Q1+..., with its binary marked product. Work in the multi-trace sector covered by the classical discriminant lemma of Kibe–van Leuven–Mathieson (in particular all gauge-invariant polynomial local jets for SU(3)). Let E1=H(Q0), and write E(r+1)=H(E(r),d(r)). There is a natural family of classical Cartan maps rho(r): E(r) -> A_C, with rho(1) the leading restriction and rho(r+1) induced on cycles, such that rho(r) d(r)=0 for every r>=1.

This does not assert that the unprojected cochain Q_r F has vanishing Cartan restriction; a higher obstruction generally contains lower-order representative corrections.

**Inputs.**

1. On E1, Cartan restriction is an algebra map because the classical differential restricts to zero. Let Phi be one scalar of fixed flavour and Delta the discriminant of its characteristic polynomial in the defining representation. rho(1)(Delta) is a nonzero polynomial in even scalar variables and is not a zero divisor in the Cartan super-polynomial algebra (nor its Weyl invariants). The pure-scalar Delta is a formal Q cocycle by flavour odd-weight counting.
2. The classical discriminant lemma says that for every polynomial Q0 cocycle F there is k>=0 and a polynomial multi-graviton expression G such that [Delta]^k [F]=[G]. See [Kibe–van Leuven–Mathieson, arXiv:2610.22332v3, Sec. 6.2, (105)–(110)](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.22332v3#S6.SS2). Its graviton class is represented by products of the classical single-trace graviton components (the weak-coupling Taylor letters and gauge-invariant relative sector are the same).
3. [Li–Liu–Chang, arXiv:2611.24160v1, Theorem 9.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24160v1#page=86) explicitly supplies an all-order closed lift of every multi-graviton polynomial by replacing products with fixed-bracketing marked products. The marked product satisfies the exact Q Leibniz rule; the induced page products and d(r) derivation are [Eq. (9.45)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24160v1#page=94). Only these properties are required. Alternatively, the specific G(d,Y) used in the discriminant lemma have fixed flavour weight and a term Tr(Phi^(d-1)Y) with Y a single jet. Hence weights in the two other flavours are each at most one. Their classical closure and [Q1]-closure from KVM (97) give a homogeneous order-one primitive, and every later term vanishes by the one-per-flavour loss per loop. This alternative needs a common normalization and representative dictionary for the two constructions.

**Page proof.** Suppose rho(r) is defined as an algebra map, which is true at r=1. For x in E(r) take an E1 representative x0 through the successive kernel/quotient operations. The classical identity in input 2 implies Delta^k x = [G]_r on E(r): multiplication on every page is formed from the product of leading classes; rho is not used in this identity. The all-order lift of G implies d(r)[G]_r=0 whenever [G]_r is defined; the class may already be zero. Similarly d(r)Delta=0. Since Delta is even, the page Leibniz rule yields Delta^k d(r)x=0. Applying rho(r) gives rho(Delta)^k rho(r)(d(r)x)=0. Cancel the non-zero-divisor to conclude rho(r)d(r)=0. Hence rho(r) on ker d(r) descends to the ring map rho(r+1). This is an induction on finite pages, with no assumption that the spectral sequence converges or that multigravitons are never hit by an incoming differential before scalar localization.

In fact, ker rho(r) is the Delta-power-torsion in E(r): apply the zero-Cartan case of the discriminant lemma to an E1 representative of a class in the kernel, then pass the relation to E(r). The converse is cancellation in A_C. If a finite partial lift through order r−1 has residual R_r at order r, R_r is the E1 leading representative of d(r) of the source page class, up to earlier boundaries. Since all earlier boundaries are invisible by the same induction, rho(R_r)=0 for any such partial lift. Thus the zero-Cartan assumptions on residuals in Table 2.2 of Li–Liu–Chang can be removed in these matching formal complexes; exact sector ranks and other hypotheses still need their own certification.

**Equivalent chain argument.** In the u-filtered cochain complex, left marked multiplication by the closed Delta is a filtered cochain map. Form the filtered direct limit over successive applications, regrading physical charges of each copy by the weight of Delta. Its classical page is E1[Delta^-1] by exactness of filtered colimits. Every class there has a fully closed lift: move its representative to a stage at which its numerator is [G]. Hence the localized sequence collapses page by page. This does not require a strictly associative product on cochains, a literal inverse cochain, or interchange of completion and localization.

**SU(3) j=30 implication.** The eight reported C-odd Coulomb-visible directions cannot carry a component of the formal d2 image in their Cartan span. It is not yet legitimate to set all of d2 to zero: there is one reported Cartan-invisible classical class. To exclude its E2 image one must show it does not survive to E2, or verify the required primary selection on E2 (including persistence modulo d1 of a nonzero lowering and the compatibility of the quantum charge action). A non-primary test in E1 alone does not prove the E2 statement. All-order non-torsion of a specific representative still requires its own proof.

**Nonperturbative scope.** No instanton Q, finite-coupling anomalous dimension, or exact physical ring is derived here. Repeating the proof across a nonperturbative filtration would require an exact filtered multiplication, a suitable weak-coupling classical page and fully closed lifts of the same generators in that common scheme. No such comparison is assumed or claimed. This note addresses only formal perturbative images, including representative corrections.

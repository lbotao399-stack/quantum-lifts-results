## 4. Pure SYM: perturbative complex and quantum product

Local operators of the holomorphic twist are generated by the even field $b$, the odd ghost $c$, and their holomorphic jets; $\partial_1,\partial_2$ denote the two holomorphic derivatives.

The trace formulas use the Chevalley basis and trace-dual basis of the calculations listed in Table E1: $c$ has Lie-algebra coordinates, while $b$ has dual coordinates, represented as a matrix through the source trace pairing.

The complex is introduced in [Semi-Chiral Operators in 4d N=1 Gauge Theories](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01039).

Its tree differential is

$$
Q_0c=c^2,\qquad Q_0b=[c,b],\qquad [Q_0,\partial_1]=[Q_0,\partial_2]=0.
$$

Terms containing undifferentiated ghosts cancel on gauge-invariant words.

The calculation retains them when checking the full identities.

$N_b,N_c$ count the $b,c$ letters.

The weight $(n_1,n_2)$ counts derivatives in the two directions, with each $b$ contributing an additional $(1,1)$.

With the source loop parameter $\hbar$, the perturbative differential is

$$
Q_{\mathrm{pert}}=\sum_{\substack{r\ge0\\\mathrm{loop\ orders}}}\hbar^rQ_r,
\qquad Q_{\mathrm{pert}}^2=0,
\qquad
Q_r:(N_b,N_c;n_1,n_2)\longmapsto(N_b-r,N_c+1;n_1,n_2).
$$

The complex at fixed weight is finite-dimensional.

**Definition 4.1 (Cohomology).**

For a square-zero differential, cocycles form its kernel and boundaries form its image.

A primitive is a cochain mapped to the specified boundary; cohomology identifies cocycles that differ by a boundary.

$$
H(Q_0)=\ker Q_0/\operatorname{im}Q_0.
$$

Its spectral-sequence pages and induced differentials are

<a id="np-f1ccd4f158d90ace-1"></a>

$$
E_1=H(Q_0),\qquad E_{r+1}=H(E_r,d_r),\qquad d_1[\mathcal O]=[Q_1\mathcal O].
$$

The first differential tests whether a tree expression admits a first loop correction.

For $Q_0\mathcal O=0$, the equation to solve is

$$
Q_0(\text{first correction})=-Q_1\mathcal O.
$$

Cartan restriction keeps the diagonal traceless matrix components and sets off-diagonal components to zero.

These diagonal matrices form the Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak t$.

The odd diagonal ghost coefficients anticommute; diagonal $b$ coefficients commute.

This restriction gives an explicit obstruction test.

> **Proof of the Cartan obstruction test.**
>
> $$
> \left.Q_0c\right|_{\mathfrak t}=\left.c^2\right|_{\mathfrak t}=0,
> \qquad
> \left.Q_0b\right|_{\mathfrak t}=\left.[c,b]\right|_{\mathfrak t}=0.
> $$
>
> The same identities hold for every derivative because $Q_0$ commutes with $\partial_1,\partial_2$.
>
> Consequently every polynomial boundary has zero Cartan restriction:
>
> $$
> \left.Q_0A\right|_{\mathfrak t}=0,
> \qquad
> Q_0(\text{first correction})=-Q_1\mathcal O
> \ \Longrightarrow\ 
> \left.Q_1\mathcal O\right|_{\mathfrak t}=0.
> $$
>
> A nonzero restricted image rules out a first correction.
>
> A zero restricted image leaves the full polynomial equation to be solved; an explicit solution establishes the first lift.
>
> $\square$

$E_\infty$ is the stable page of this finite filtration, and $[\mathcal O]$ denotes the corresponding cohomology class.

In the trace convention used here, the gaugino bilinear is

$$
C_0=\operatorname{tr}(\partial_1c\,\partial_2c),\qquad Q_{\mathrm{pert}}C_0=0.
$$

$Q_1$ is a second-order differential operator acting on pairs of letters, so ordinary multiplication acquires loop corrections.

Write $\star$ for the source quantum product, $\mu_r$ for its $r$-loop coefficient, and $|A|$ for the Grassmann parity of $A$.

Then

$$
A\star B=AB+\sum_{\substack{r\ge1\\\mathrm{loop\ orders}}}\hbar^r\mu_r(A,B),
$$

$$
Q_{\mathrm{pert}}(A\star B)
=(Q_{\mathrm{pert}}A)\star B+(-1)^{|A|}A\star(Q_{\mathrm{pert}}B).
$$

We use [Theorem 5.4 of the all-loop manuscript](../sources/all-loop/theorems.md#b-00513) as an external input: when the interaction satisfies the quantum master equation, the induced cohomology product is associative, graded commutative, and unital.

Pure SYM satisfies the source gauge-anomaly cancellation condition.

The product-kernel appendix states its application and the one-loop and two-loop kernels.

The following calculations evaluate finite-$N$ products, representatives, and primitives in this perturbative Ward/holomorphic model.

### SU(2): hidden product and exact nilpotency

In the product convention of Table E1, the leading coefficients of $C_2$ and $C_{4,\mathrm{source}}$ are

$$
[\hbar^0]C_2=-\operatorname{tr}(b^2)C_0,
\qquad
[\hbar^0]C_{4,\mathrm{source}}=-\operatorname{tr}(b^2)^2C_0.
$$

$[\hbar^r]$ extracts the coefficient of $\hbar^r$.

The manuscript uses $C_0[\hbar^0]C_4=([\hbar^0]C_2)^2$, giving the exact conversion between the saved representatives:

$$
C_4=-C_{4,\mathrm{source}}.
$$

The operator $C_2$ is a polynomial representative with leading term $-\operatorname{tr}(b^2)C_0$ and two loop corrections.

The class $[C_2]$ identifies all representatives obtained by adding $Q_{\mathrm{pert}}A$, where $A$ is an allowed local polynomial.

Thus a representative is an expression in fields, while its class records that expression modulo these additions.

$$
C_2=-\operatorname{tr}(b^2)C_0+\hbar[\hbar]C_2+\hbar^2[\hbar^2]C_2,
\qquad
[C_2+Q_{\mathrm{pert}}A]=[C_2].
$$

A nonzero class means that the equation $C_2=Q_{\mathrm{pert}}A$ has no solution in the specified local polynomial complex.

Closure of the representative is the following finite system.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_0([\hbar^0]C_2)&=0,\\
Q_0([\hbar]C_2)+Q_1([\hbar^0]C_2)&=0,\\
Q_0([\hbar^2]C_2)+Q_1([\hbar]C_2)+Q_2([\hbar^0]C_2)&=0.
\end{aligned}
$$

> **Proof of the closure criterion.**
>
> The coefficient $[\hbar^s]C_2$ has $N_b=2-s$, for $s=0,1,2$.
>
> Since $Q_r$ lowers $N_b$ by $r$,
>
> $$
> Q_r([\hbar^s]C_2)=0\qquad(r+s>2).
> $$
>
> Expanding $Q_{\mathrm{pert}}C_2$ therefore gives exactly the three displayed equations.
>
> Adding a boundary preserves closure because
>
> $$
> Q_{\mathrm{pert}}(C_2+Q_{\mathrm{pert}}A)
> =Q_{\mathrm{pert}}C_2+Q_{\mathrm{pert}}^2A=0.
> $$
>
> $\square$

**Proposition P1.**

In this article sign convention, the full perturbative cohomology satisfies

$$
[C_0][C_2]=0,\qquad [C_2]^2=-3[C_0][C_4]\ne0.
$$

> **Proof.**
>
> The complete primitive for the first relation is $w_2+\hbar w_1+\hbar^2w_0$, listed in Table E1; each subscript denotes its $N_b$ value.
>
> The loop coefficients satisfy
>
> $$
> Q_0w_2=C_0[\hbar^0]C_2,
> $$
>
> $$
> Q_0w_1+Q_1w_2=[\hbar](C_0\star C_2),
> $$
>
> $$
> Q_0w_0+Q_1w_1+Q_2w_2=[\hbar^2](C_0\star C_2).
> $$
>
> The three rational polynomials contain $24,221,105$ canonical component monomials, respectively; the corresponding full-product coefficients contain $6,75,135$.
>
> Every polynomial coefficient agrees in each identity.
>
> Since $Q_r$ lowers $N_b$ by exactly $r$, the higher equations vanish term by term.
>
> Thus
>
> $$
> C_0\star C_2=Q_{\mathrm{pert}}(w_2+\hbar w_1+\hbar^2w_0).
> $$
>
> The rational primitive for the second relation is the `boundary` field of `full_hidden_product_exact.pkl` in Table E1.
>

>

> =Q_{\mathrm{pert}}(\text{full product primitive in Table E1}).
> $$
>
> For nonvanishing, the first two components of $C_0\star C_4$ can be removed successively.

>

>

>

>
> $\square$

**Corollary P2.**

The nilpotency index of $[C_2]$ is exactly $3$.

> **Proof.**
>
> Apply associativity and graded commutativity in the cohomology covered by the external ring theorem:
>
> $$
> [C_2]^3=-3[C_0][C_4][C_2]
> =-3([C_0][C_2])[C_4]=0,
> \qquad [C_2]^2\ne0.
> $$
>
> Both full boundary identities in Table E1 hold over $\mathbb Q[\hbar]$, and hence over $\mathbb Q[[\hbar]]$.
>
> The calculation uses polynomial identities in $\hbar$ directly and remains valid in the presence of $\hbar$-torsion.
>
> $\square$

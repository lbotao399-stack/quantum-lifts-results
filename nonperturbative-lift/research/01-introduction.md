## Introduction

A supersymmetry transformation relates bosonic and fermionic fields; we study operators preserved by one chosen transformation and identify operators whose difference is a variation under that transformation.

Perturbation theory expands this transformation in a formal loop-counting parameter; a class survives when its representative can be corrected to satisfy the invariance equation at each order and remains distinct from a variation.

A change of the equivalence classes, a change of their multiplication rule, a change of the allowed vacuum expectation values, and a change of an interaction coefficient are four distinct questions.

A gauge transformation changes the field description while leaving observables invariant; the group $SU(N)$ consists of unitary $N\times N$ matrices with determinant one, with $N$ the matrix size.

For $SU(2)$, we give an operator, denoted $C_2$ below, whose equivalence class has a nonzero square and vanishing cube under the quantum product.

For $SU(3)$, the operators $C_3,C_4$ are given with every correction required in their specified degrees, so that the full perturbative invariance equation can be checked.

For $SU(2),SU(3),SU(4)$, finitely many independent trace expressions generate the leading operators when multiplied by arbitrary polynomials in the invariant traces.

At tree level, before loop corrections, this list gives every leading operator in the stated sector and proves its independent generators.

For $SU(4)$, we also solve the necessary first-loop equations on diagonal matrices; the full field-space correction has been constructed in the degrees stated in the results chapter.

Evaluating the fields on diagonal matrices reduces the first correction equation to explicit polynomial tests, whose solutions can be written as contour integrals.

When a product is a supersymmetry variation, an explicit operator producing that variation can be combined with another such operator to construct a new preserved class.

An exact identity relating repeated multiplication to the full supersymmetry transformation then determines how a nonperturbative correction acts on these newly constructed classes.

We calculate the correlation function at distinct points, the gauge-field transport that compares color-carrying operator factors at those points, the regular field responses to an inserted source, and the variation of the integration variables describing an instanton.

The connection contribution varies the gauge field inside the partner operator’s covariant derivatives; the full physical coefficient also contains the integration measure and local terms produced when operator insertions coincide.

Supersymmetric Yang–Mills theory contains a gauge field and its fermionic partner; supersymmetric quantum chromodynamics also contains matter fields transforming under the gauge group.

In a dual description with the same low-energy observables, a nonzero matter expectation value completely breaks the magnetic gauge group; on this region of field space, the effective field description gives an instanton contribution that makes a determinant operator a supersymmetry variation.

At nonzero matter mass, we construct the polynomial operator in the original field variables that enters this proposed pairing; its local instanton transformation is the remaining matching equation.

The determinant generates the entire ideal of effective scalars removed in these magnetic coordinates. In a second magnetic description of the same confined theory, fifteen quadratic relations generate the removed ideal. An exact change of variables on the nonzero-weight fibers relates the interactions and is singular at zero weight. In the massless microscopic theory a pair of color evaluations proves that all sixteen flavor components of an incoming current survive every power of the loop parameter.

In the flat-space holomorphic-topological theory with eight supercharges, translation constrains any putative instanton differential. Solving every invariant bilinear cycle of a simple gauge algebra gives the entire possible incoming space for a three-fermion family. Translation then protects both families at every order of a bulk deformation preserving the stated charges.

Solving the equations of massive fields explains inverse-field interactions and multiple algebraic branches, with the allowed denominator stated before division.

Interactions with additional fermions, exact contour integrals determining low-energy couplings, and corrections to operator scaling dimensions give further nonperturbative observables.

The next chapter states the results with their definitions and assumptions; the subsequent chapters give the calculations.

A proved statement includes its operator space, parameter domain, and computed perturbative order.

A conditional statement displays the additional equation that must hold for its conclusion to follow.

The open questions concern the remaining microscopic coefficients and the extension of computed operator families to higher degrees.

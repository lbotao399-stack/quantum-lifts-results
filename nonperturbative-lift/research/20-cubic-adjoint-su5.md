## 20. Cubic-adjoint $SU(5)$: perturbative survival of a nilpotent scalar

<a id="np-0c6be64b76927f71-1"></a>

### The full classical invariant quotient

Take the Kutasov–Schwimmer–Seiberg theory with $SU(5)$ and $N_f=4$.

The electric adjoint $X$ is a traceless matrix.

Its source superpotential is

$$
W=\frac{s_0}{3}\operatorname{Tr}X^3
+\frac{s_1}{2}\operatorname{Tr}X^2.
$$

At $s_1=0$ and $s_0\ne0$, write the traceless F-term matrix as $F(X)$:

$$
F(X)=X^2-\frac{\operatorname{Tr}X^2}{5}I.
$$

The ideal $(F)$ is generated by its matrix entries and retains nilpotents.

The notation $\mathbb C[\mathfrak{sl}_5]$ denotes polynomials in a traceless complex five-by-five matrix.

Dividing by $(F)$ identifies two polynomials when their difference is a polynomial combination of F-equations.

The superscript $SU(5)$ retains gauge-invariant classes.

A nilpotent class is nonzero although some positive power of it vanishes.

Evaluating on ordinary vacuum values can therefore miss such classes.

The exact invariant quotient is

$$
\left(\mathbb C[\mathfrak{sl}_5]/(F)\right)^{SU(5)}
=\frac{\mathbb C[\operatorname{Tr}X^2]}{((\operatorname{Tr}X^2)^3)},
\qquad [ (\operatorname{Tr}X^2)^2]\ne0.
$$

> **Proof.**
>
> The conjugation-invariant polynomials are freely generated by $\operatorname{Tr}X^2,\operatorname{Tr}X^3,\operatorname{Tr}X^4,\operatorname{Tr}X^5$.
>
> On the dense cyclic locus, polynomial matrix covariants expand in $I,X,\ldots,X^4$.
>
> Evaluation on the companion matrix makes the coefficients polynomial in the characteristic coefficients, extending the expansion to all $X$.
>
> Reynolds averaging therefore reduces the full invariant ideal to the generators $\operatorname{Tr}(X^jF)$ for $j=1,2,3,4$.
>
> Newton and Cayley–Hamilton identities give
>
> $$
> \operatorname{Tr}X^6
> =\frac34\operatorname{Tr}X^2\operatorname{Tr}X^4
> +\frac13(\operatorname{Tr}X^3)^2
> -\frac18(\operatorname{Tr}X^2)^3,
> $$
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \operatorname{Tr}(XF)&=\operatorname{Tr}X^3,\\
> \operatorname{Tr}(X^2F)&=\operatorname{Tr}X^4-\frac15(\operatorname{Tr}X^2)^2,\\
> \operatorname{Tr}(X^3F)&=\operatorname{Tr}X^5-\frac15\operatorname{Tr}X^2\operatorname{Tr}X^3.
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> The final generator satisfies
>
> $$
> \operatorname{Tr}(X^4F)
> -\frac{11}{20}\operatorname{Tr}X^2\operatorname{Tr}(X^2F)
> -\frac13\operatorname{Tr}X^3\operatorname{Tr}(XF)
> =-\frac3{200}(\operatorname{Tr}X^2)^3.
> $$
>
> The full ideal is therefore
>
> $$
> (\operatorname{Tr}X^5,
> \operatorname{Tr}X^4-(\operatorname{Tr}X^2)^2/5,
> \operatorname{Tr}X^3,
> (\operatorname{Tr}X^2)^3).
> $$
>
> The reduced Gröbner basis has standard monomials $1,\operatorname{Tr}X^2,(\operatorname{Tr}X^2)^2$.
>
> $\square$

Ordinary complex solutions satisfy $\operatorname{Tr}X^2=0$: a nonzero value would give $X$ two opposite eigenvalues whose multiplicities cannot be equal in dimension five.

The reduced invariant quotient is therefore a point, while the full quotient retains a nonzero nilpotent square.

The matrix variety itself also contains nonzero square-zero matrices.

### Detector in the full perturbative Ward complex

Here $\gamma$ is the source antichiral scalar letter.

For derivative-free interactions, each edge of an $n$-loop rooted graph consumes an antifield terminal.

A pure scalar root supplies zero, so

$$
\#(\mathsf b,\beta\text{ output})
=(n+1-\#(\bar W\text{ vertices}))-(2n+1)
=-n-\#(\bar W\text{ vertices}).
$$

For $n\ge1$, such a contraction is impossible.

Gauge-invariant pure scalar polynomials therefore have vanishing outgoing positive-loop action.

The source local kernel obeys the uniform coordinate dilation identity

$$
I_\Gamma[\lambda/t;tw]=t^{-2n}I_\Gamma[\lambda;w].
$$

Here $\lambda$ are output momenta, $w$ are input shifts, and $t>0$ is the dilation parameter.

The output derivative degree of a Taylor coefficient exceeds its input degree by $2n$.

The source current representation gives an increase of $n$ in each holomorphic derivative total.

Let $\pi$ set positive jets, ghosts, and antifields to zero, then pass to the classical F-term quotient:

$$
\pi\mathbf Q_{\mathrm{pert}}=0,
\qquad
\pi((\operatorname{Tr}\gamma^2)^2)\ne0.
$$

> **Proof: nonexactness and absence of $\hbar$-torsion.**
>
> Suppose a cochain $U$ and a nonnegative integer $r$ satisfy
>
> $$
> \mathbf Q_{\mathrm{pert}}U=\hbar^r(\operatorname{Tr}\gamma^2)^2,
> $$
>
> Evaluation gives
>
> $$
> 0=\hbar^r\pi((\operatorname{Tr}\gamma^2)^2)\ne0.
> $$
>
> Thus the scalar square is closed, nonexact, and free of $\hbar$-torsion in the specified full perturbative local Ward complex.
>
> $\square$

The detector establishes survival of this representative.

Products vanishing in the classical F-term quotient may acquire positive-jet Konishi corrections.

A multiplicative injection of the full classical quotient into the quantum ring therefore requires a separate product calculation.

### Kutasov trace-sector prediction and full-ring matching

At generic $s_1\ne0$, the adjoint critical-point multiplicities $r_1+r_2=5$ give

$$
c_1=\frac{s_1r_2}{s_0(r_1-r_2)},
\qquad
c_2=-\frac{s_1r_1}{s_0(r_1-r_2)}.
$$

The choices $(r_1,r_2)=(5,0),(4,1),(3,2)$ give, respectively,

$$
\langle\operatorname{Tr}X^2\rangle
=0,\quad \frac{20}{9}(s_1/s_0)^2,\quad30(s_1/s_0)^2.
$$

The magnetic adjoint is $Y\in\mathfrak{sl}_3$.

The source trace map has a positive sign:

$$
\operatorname{Tr}X^2
=\frac53\operatorname{Tr}Y^2+\frac{20}{9}(s_1/s_0)^2.
$$

Its magnetic characteristic relation implies the source trace-sector relation

$$
\left[\operatorname{Tr}X^2-\frac{20}{9}(s_1/s_0)^2\right]
\left[\operatorname{Tr}X^2-30(s_1/s_0)^2\right]=0.
$$

For $s_1\ne0$, this excludes the $(5,0)$ branch.

The low-energy $SU(5)$ theory with $N_f=4$ on that branch has ADS term $\Lambda_L^{11}/\det M$, independently giving the same vacuum exclusion.

At $s_1=0$, the source presentation predicts $(\operatorname{Tr}X^2)^2=0$, strengthening the classical cubic nilpotence.

KSS restrict this argument to the mesonic origin.

Extending it to the full renormalized ring requires compatible characteristic, Konishi, and trace-map prescriptions.

[Kutasov–Schwimmer–Seiberg, §2.3, §6.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9510222)

The quantum characteristic function supplies a concrete diagnostic.

In Ferrari–Wens notation, $u_j=\operatorname{Tr}Y^j$, $F(z)$ is the characteristic function, and $U(z)$ is the flavor-mass determinant:

$$
F(z)=z^3\exp\left(-\frac{u_2}{2z^2}-\frac{u_3}{3z^3}-\frac{u_4}{4z^4}+\cdots\right),
\qquad
U(z)=\det\left[\frac{s_0}{\mu^2}(M_1z+M_2)\right].
$$

Here $M_1,M_2$ are dynamical meson singlets; $\bar\Lambda$ denotes the KSS magnetic scale, with the bar serving as a magnetic label.

Assume its quantum characteristic equation holds in the same nonreduced operator prescription:

$$
F(z)+\frac{\bar\Lambda^2U(z)}{F(z)}=H(z),
\qquad \deg H=3,
$$

Extracting the coefficient $[z^j]$ gives

$$
[z^{-1}]F=\frac{u_2^2}{8}-\frac{u_4}{4},
\qquad
[z^{-1}]\frac{U}{F}=[z^2]U+\frac{u_2}{2}[z^4]U,
$$

$$
u_4=\frac12u_2^2+4\bar\Lambda^2[z^2]U
+2\bar\Lambda^2u_2[z^4]U.
$$

If compatible anomaly equations also give $u_4=u_2^2/3$, then

$$
u_2^2+24\bar\Lambda^2[z^2]U
+12\bar\Lambda^2u_2[z^4]U=0.
$$

The final two terms vanish at the mesonic origin.

Away from that locus, their singlet relations require examination.

This coefficient calculation is conditional; whether the two terms are nonzero in the full theory remains open.

Their engineering dimensions and conserved R-charges agree with those of $u_2^2$: $4$ and $8/3$, respectively.

[Ferrari](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0701220); [Ferrari–Wens](https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.2978)

In a compatible operator prescription, the singlet F-equations give $\widetilde qq=\widetilde qYq=0$.

The adjoint variation $\delta Y=qM_1\widetilde q-\operatorname{Tr}(qM_1\widetilde q)I/3$ has vanishing Jacobian and gives $M_1\widetilde qY^2q=0$.

The fundamental Konishi variation $\delta q=Yq$ then implies $\operatorname{Tr}(W_\alpha W^\alpha Y)=0$.

This handles the dressed-glueball term.

Higher-trace definitions and dynamical singlet contributions still require joint matching.

### Scalar chiral-ring zero and one-supercharge cohomology

In the same unitary $\mathcal N=1$ SCFT, use the Dolan–Osborn normalization $Q=\bar Q_1$ and $Q^+=-\bar S^1$, with radial adjoint denoted by $+$:

$$
\{Q,Q^+\}=2(H-2\bar J_3-3R/2).
$$

Here $H$ is the dilation generator, and $\bar J_3$ is the dotted-spin Cartan generator.

Let $|O\rangle$ be a Lorentz scalar state closed under both $\bar Q$ components, with dimension and exact R-charge $\Delta,r$.

> **Proof.**
>
> For $2\Delta-3r>0$,
>
> $$
> |O\rangle=-\frac{\bar Q_1\bar S^1|O\rangle}{2\Delta-3r}.
> $$
>
> For $2\Delta-3r=0$, positivity gives $(\bar Q_1)^+|O\rangle=(\bar Q_2)^+|O\rangle=0$.
>
> Vanishing in the full chiral ring means $|O\rangle=\bar Q_{\dot\alpha}|X^{\dot\alpha}\rangle$, so
>
> $$
> \langle O|O\rangle
> =\langle O|\bar Q_{\dot\alpha}|X^{\dot\alpha}\rangle=0.
> $$
>
> Unitarity identifies this with the zero state; its chosen one-supercharge class also vanishes.
>
> $\square$

This conclusion requires the actual full scalar operator relation and the correct RG representative.

[Dolan–Osborn](https://arxiv.org/abs/0801.4947) The conserved UV assignment in the Kutasov example gives $R(M_1)=1/3$.

Interpreting this as the exact SCFT primary charge would give $\Delta(M_1)=1/2<1$.

The standard free-meson resolution instead gives $R(M_1)=2/3$ and $\Delta(M_1)=1$.

At magnetic $R(Y)=2/3$ and $R(q)=R(\widetilde q)=1/2$, the term $M_1\widetilde qYq$ has R-charge $7/3$ and, if its primary survives, dimension $7/2$.

The coupling then has dimension $-1/2$.

Comparing operators of the full UV superpotential with those of the strict fixed-point superpotential requires retaining this irrelevant-coupling issue.

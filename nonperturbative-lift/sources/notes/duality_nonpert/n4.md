# N=4 SYM: what the primary sources establish

Checked 2026-11-13. This is a literature assessment, not a new calculation. **None of the N=4 papers examined here establishes an instanton lifting of a class already certified to survive the complete perturbative differential.** This statement concerns these sources, not an exhaustive absence theorem.

## 1. The S-duality mismatch is now a perturbative example

Chang–Lin, [2510.24008, §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24008#S3), compare full classical local-operator cohomologies, including derivative sectors, for SO(7) and USp(6). At their level 18, SO(7) has two additional classes. In their charge notation, J1,J2 are angular momenta and q1,q2,q3 are R-charges:

$$
(J_1,J_2,q_1,q_2,q_3)
=\left(\frac12,\frac12,\frac52,\frac52,\frac52\right),
\qquad (0,0,3,3,3).
$$

The first is fermionic and fortuitous; the second is bosonic, monotone, and in the 1/8-BPS chiral ring. Their classical cohomology counts are 124 versus 123, and 85 versus 84. Their index contributions cancel. Assuming exact S-duality, classical exactness fails; that argument alone allowed either perturbative or nonperturbative resolution.

Choi–Lee, [Konishi lifts a black hole, 2511.09519, §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.09519#S3), subsequently supply the resolution. Their Q0 is classical, QK the first quantum term from the generalized Konishi anomaly, Of the fortuitous class, Om its monotone partner, and Lambda a primitive for the remaining classical exact term:

$$
Q_K O_f=O_m+Q_0\Lambda,
\qquad [Q_K O_f]=[O_m]\neq0\quad\text{in }H(Q_0).
$$

This is their displayed normalization of Om; it must not be silently identified with an independently normalized trace polynomial. They check against the complete 741-dimensional exact subspace in the target sector. QK carries gYM squared divided by 32 pi squared in their Eq. (2.5). It differentiates two letters, so the classical Leibniz argument does not survive. **This is quantum perturbative class pairing, not a purely instantonic lift.**

## 2. The complete first quantum differential is available

Kasia Budzik–Justin Kulp, [2512.07771v3, Eqs. (3), (10), (12), Appendix B](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.07771), use hbar as loop-counting parameter:

$$
\mathcal Q=Q_{\mathrm{free}}+Q_{\mathrm{tree}}
+\hbar Q_{1\text{-loop}}+\hbar^2Q_{2\text{-loop}}+\cdots.
$$

For their adjoint-valued fermionic superfield C on C^{2|3}, theta1,theta2,theta3 are the odd coordinates and the dotted derivatives are holomorphic derivatives. With component labels A,B,C,D,E and index variance supplied by the source invariant color pairing, their Eq. (10) reads

$$
Q_1\bigl(C^A(\theta)C^B(\theta')\bigr)
=-\frac12 f^A{}_{CD}f^{BC}{}_{E}
(\theta_1-\theta'_1)(\theta_2-\theta'_2)(\theta_3-\theta'_3)
\partial_{\dot\alpha}C^D(\theta)
\partial^{\dot\alpha}C^E(\theta').
$$

The source spinor pairing raises the dotted derivative index. Derivative-input extensions are in Appendix B. The factor 1/2 includes the v3 correction. The computation uses holomorphic BRST anomalies and higher operations of an L-infinity conformal algebra.

Their Q0 includes the classical differential. On the chain space,

$$
Q_1^2+\{Q_0,Q_2\}=0.
$$

Q1 generally does not square to zero there, although its induced action on H(Q0) does. Thus a complete Q1 is not an all-loop result. Footnote 8 gives an upper bound Q_(n+1) for possible corrections to a length-n operator; individual higher terms depend on scheme.

## 3. A recent exact perturbative coefficient

Kibe–van Leuven–Mathieson, [2610.22332v3, §5.2, Eqs. (80)–(81)](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.22332#S5.SS2), set S=Tr(lambda+ lambda-), with lambda+ and lambda- the two gaugino components. Their F4 is a fortuitous source; kappaQ is the nonzero overall coefficient fixed in their pair-action Eq. (65). Their restriction rhoJ retains four specified root directions; Omega is the ordered product of the resulting eight Grassmann variables. They compute

$$
\rho_J(Q_1F_4)=-4608\kappa_Q\Omega,
\qquad
\rho_J(\mathcal S^4)=384\Omega,
$$

$$
[Q_1][F_4]=-12\kappa_Q[\mathcal S^4]\neq0.
$$

The target cohomology is one-dimensional, so this detector determines the full pairing. It is a descendant of the earlier SO(7) example, not an instanton result. Their source can have nonzero Cartan restriction: vanishing of its trace relation on the Cartan does not imply vanishing of the source itself. Their higher-rank proposal still requires constructing the derivative completion.

## 4. What survives the first correction

Seunggyu Kim–Jehyun Lee–Siyul Lee–Jaewon Song, [Black hole cohomologies in N=4 SYM, 2610.20340v2, §4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.20340#S4.SS2), prove first-quantum-order protection of the SU(3) F1 brane-fused tower. Their first two operators are O30^(3), O32^(3), in SU(3) R-symmetry representations [3,0], [4,0]. They check their Q1 images are classical Q-exact and extend this to dressing by the scalar graviton generators u2,u3.

Their dBMN is a nonnegative grading; Q1 raises it by one. At dBMN=0 there is therefore no incoming Q1 differential. Both closure and non-exactness survive this first page. This does not establish survival under Q2, higher transferred differentials, or instantons. The charge-conjugation pattern is tested and conjectured; it is not a general nonperturbative theorem.

## 5. Protection of correlators is a different claim

Baggio–de Boer–Papadodimas, [1203.1036, §§5–8](https://arxiv.org/html/1203.1036), prove nonperturbative nonrenormalization of three-point functions of half-BPS primaries, extremal half-BPS correlators, and three-point functions with one quarter-BPS and two half-BPS operators. Their proof uses superconformal Ward identities and shortening, for arbitrary gauge group. Section 8 explicitly separates the question of short-multiplet recombination from these correlator results. Thus these theorems do not prove that every classical 1/16-BPS class remains present.

D'Hoker–Heslop–Howe–Ryzhov, [hep-th/0301104](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-th/0301104), distinguish quarter-BPS primaries from long-multiplet descendants with identical classical charges. Their constructed combinations are orthogonal to descendants; the explicit dimension check is at order g squared. Classical annihilation by several supercharges alone does not identify the quantum primary.

## 6. A genuine instanton correction that is not BPS lifting

Alday–Korchemsky, [1605.06346v2, §2.4, Eq. (31)](https://arxiv.org/html/1605.06346#S2.SS4), use the Yang–Mills coupling g and vacuum angle theta, with

$$
\tau=\frac{\theta}{2\pi}+\frac{4\pi i}{g^2},
\qquad e^{2\pi i\tau}=e^{-8\pi^2/g^2+i\theta}.
$$

Their exact leading one-instanton plus anti-instanton term in the SU(2) Konishi anomalous dimension is

$$
\left.\gamma_K\right|_{\text{leading one-instanton and anti-instanton term}}
=-\frac95\left(\frac{g^2}{4\pi^2}\right)^2
\left(e^{2\pi i\tau}+e^{-2\pi i\bar\tau}\right).
$$

This equality selects the stated term, not the complete anomalous dimension. Konishi is already an unprotected multiplet. The same paper computes instanton changes to its OPE coefficient with two half-BPS operators. Neither calculation removes an all-orders perturbative BPS class. Their method exploits different components of one supermultiplet, saturates the sixteen exact fermion modes, and extracts an anomalous dimension from the logarithmic small-instanton divergence. A leading zero-mode-counting zero is not an all-orders nonrenormalization proof.

## 7. A viable next N=4 target

Start with the explicit SU(3) O30^(3) and O32^(3) representatives and construct their complete fixed-charge complexes, including non-BMN targets and all allowed partner classes. Use the complete quantum supercharge, homotopy transfer, and the finite correction bound to certify the full perturbative answer. Charge conjugation can exclude particular partners but does not replace that computation.

Within this local-operator complex, only a surviving class with an allowed partner is a candidate for an instanton lifting calculation. Then evaluate an instanton-sector anomalous mixing or supercharge matrix element, including operator mixing, zero-mode saturation, and contact terms. A nonzero correlator correction alone is insufficient. If no partner exists in the complete perturbative spectrum, pairing inside this complex is excluded; extending that conclusion to the full theory requires justifying the nonperturbative completeness of the operator space.

The solved SO(7) pair is a useful normalization and implementation benchmark, not an open instanton target. The meaningful open N=4 question is whether an all-orders perturbative survivor can be paired by a nonperturbative differential; the papers above do not answer it.

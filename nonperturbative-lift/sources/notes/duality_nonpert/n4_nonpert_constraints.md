# N=4 nonperturbative lifting: exact constraints

Checked 2026-11-13. Sources were reopened. This supplements `n4.md`; it does not modify the manuscript.

## 1. Pure-scalar SU(N) classes are detected by exact vacua

Use the three traceless complex adjoint scalars X,Y,Z of the N=1 presentation of N=4 SYM. The scalar F-term relations are

$$
[X,Y]=[Y,Z]=[Z,X]=0.
$$

Here C[X,Y,Z] means the polynomial coordinate algebra of the matrix entries; the denominator below is generated by the entries of the three commutators, without taking its radical.

[Vaccarino, math/0602660v2, Theorem 3](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0602660) proves, in characteristic zero, that the invariant quotient for **any number m of commuting matrices** is isomorphic to the ring of multisymmetric functions. This is a scheme-level assertion, including the possibly nonreduced commuting scheme before taking invariants. Its conclusion is stronger than an identification of geometric points.

For the SU(N) specialization, split each unrestricted matrix into its traceless part and scalar identity part. For X, with I_N the identity matrix,

$$
X=\left(X-\frac{\operatorname{Tr}X}{N}I_N\right)
+\frac{\operatorname{Tr}X}{N}I_N,
$$

$$
[X,Y]=\left[X-\frac{\operatorname{Tr}X}{N}I_N,
Y-\frac{\operatorname{Tr}Y}{N}I_N\right].
$$

The three trace coordinates are independent polynomial factors. Conjugation by GL(N,C) and SL(N,C) has the same action on matrices. Removing the trace factors therefore gives

$$
\left(\frac{\mathbb C[X,Y,Z]}
{([X,Y],[Y,Z],[Z,X])}\right)^{SL(N,\mathbb C)}
\cong
\left(
\frac{\mathbb C[x_1,\ldots,x_N,y_1,\ldots,y_N,z_1,\ldots,z_N]}
{\left(
\sum_{\substack{i=1,\ldots,N\\\mathrm{eigenvalues}}}x_i,
\sum_{\substack{i=1,\ldots,N\\\mathrm{eigenvalues}}}y_i,
\sum_{\substack{i=1,\ldots,N\\\mathrm{eigenvalues}}}z_i
\right)}
\right)^{S_N}.
$$

The variables x_i,y_i,z_i are the simultaneous diagonal entries, i is an external eigenvalue label, and S_N simultaneously permutes the N triples. The algebra before S_N invariants on the right is a polynomial algebra in 3N−3 variables; its invariant subalgebra is reduced. Consequently every nonzero scalar class has a nonzero value on a diagonal vacuum. No nilpotent scalar invariant is missed by this detector.

The N=4 vacuum geometry receives no quantum corrections: [Argyres–Bourget–Martone, 1904.10969, §2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/1904.10969#S2.SS3). In the two-scalar slice, this also follows from the N=2 Higgs-branch nonrenormalization argument: the gauge coupling is a background vector-multiplet parameter and cannot enter the hypermultiplet metric [Argyres–Plesser–Seiberg, hep-th/9603042, section headed “Quantum Higgs Branches and the Non-Renormalization Theorem”](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-th/9603042).

For an exact scalar chiral representative O of a nonzero class, choose a supersymmetric vacuum v on which its value is nonzero. Q is the preserved nilpotent supercharge; V would be a local primitive. The vacuum Ward identity gives

$$
O=[Q,V\}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\langle O\rangle_v=\langle[Q,V\}\rangle_v=0,
$$

$$
\langle O\rangle_v\ne0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
[O]\ne0\text{ in the exact }Q\text{-cohomology}.
$$

The bracket is the graded commutator. Scalar chiral operators admit Q-closed renormalized representatives; the nonzero vacuum function excludes their becoming exact. The invariant-theory identification establishes that this detector covers every nonzero class of the displayed scalar quotient. Thus the **pure-scalar SU(N) sector**, including its half-, quarter-, and one-eighth-BPS components, cannot be removed by an instanton-only differential.

This is protection of classes with suitable renormalized representatives, not protection of every bare ordered trace word. [Berenstein, hep-th/0507203, §3](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-th/0507203#S3) explicitly makes this distinction and separates additional gaugino generators. The argument does not cover gauginos, derivatives, general 1/16-BPS operators, or nilpotent classes in other theories. It does not apply to the SU(5) N=1 cubic-superpotential ring C[Tr X²]/((Tr X²)³), whose nonzero nilpotents vanish on all ordinary vacua.

## 2. Absolute representation protection is narrower

In [Córdova–Dumitrescu–Intriligator, 1612.00809, §2.2.4](https://arxiv.org/html/1612.00809), R_1,R_2,R_3 are SU(4)_R Dynkin labels; [j;bar j] are Lorentz Dynkin labels; Delta is scaling dimension. L denotes long shortening type, A_2 scalar semishort type, B_1 BPS type, with a bar for the opposite chirality. The absolutely protected scalar multiplets are

$$
B_1\overline B_1[0;0]_{R_1+R_2+R_3}^{(R_1,R_2,R_3)},
\qquad R_1=R_3\in\{0,1\}.
$$

The R_1=R_3=0 family is half-BPS. The source HTML is v1 and numbers this as (2.57); later PDF numbering differs. For quarter-BPS labels (q,p,q), q≥2, an allowed recombination channel exists. The singlet example is the limit Delta→2 from above:

$$
\begin{aligned}
L\overline L[0;0]_{\Delta}^{(0,0,0)}\longrightarrow{}&
A_2\overline A_2[0;0]_{2}^{(0,0,0)}
\oplus B_1\overline A_2[0;0]_{3}^{(2,0,0)}\\
&\oplus A_2\overline B_1[0;0]_{3}^{(0,0,2)}
\oplus B_1\overline B_1[0;0]_{4}^{(2,0,2)}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore arbitrary classically short scalar expressions can be long-multiplet descendants. This does not contradict section 1: the surviving scalar quotient is protected by dynamics and exact vacuum detection, beyond representation theory alone.

## 3. Correlator protection does not replace a recombination argument

[Baggio–de Boer–Papadodimas, 1203.1036, §§7–8](https://arxiv.org/html/1203.1036) separate their nonperturbative correlator theorems from the question whether short multiplets combine. An R-singlet marginal deformation preserves R quantum numbers but does not remove the channel in section 2. The product of an operator with its conjugate can contain an R-singlet; singletness of the deformation alone does not make its matrix element vanish.

[Bissi–Fardelli–Puerta-Ramisa, 2610.03606v1, §§5–6](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03606#S5) prove protection for extremal and next-to-extremal quarter-BPS four-point correlators. For external SU(4)_R labels [q_i,p_i,q_i], their exact condition is

$$
p_1+2q_1=p_2+2q_2+p_3+2q_3+p_4+2q_4-2k,
\qquad k\in\{0,1\}.
$$

The proof uses analytic superspace, harmonic analyticity, short/semishort OPE content, and Lagrangian-insertion constraints. Its input operators are protected analytic multiplets. This does not establish that every perturbative 1/16-BPS class continues to belong to that input space.

[Niarchos, 1807.04296, §§1–3](https://arxiv.org/html/1807.04296) proves flatness of the bundle of N=2 Higgs-branch primaries. The Berry-bundle construction explicitly assumes locally no level crossings. Flatness is not by itself a proof identifying formal cusp cohomology with exact finite-coupling cohomology.

## 4. Sixteen exact fermion modes give a selection rule, not a universal no-go

In [Alday–Korchemsky, 1605.06346v2, §§2.1–2.6](https://arxiv.org/html/1605.06346), an SU(N) one-instanton has 8N fermion modes: 16 exact supersymmetry/superconformal modes, and 8N−16 modes that the action can absorb. The exact-mode integral selects degree sixteen in the complete integrand. Vanishing of the lowest field profile is insufficient.

For their Konishi operator K, K^(8) denotes its component of fermion degree eight; x_0 and rho are the instanton center and size; [zeta(x)]^8 is their ordered product of eight exact modes. They obtain

$$
K^{(4)}=0,
\qquad
K^{(8)}(x)=-9\cdot2^{15}
\frac{\rho^6}{[\rho^2+(x-x_0)^2]^6}[\zeta(x)]^8.
$$

The two-point insertion K^(8)(x_1)K^(8)(x_2) saturates sixteen modes. The scalar descendant K'=g^(−4) tr([Z,X][Z,X]) has a vanishing leading semiclassical answer but acquires the same nonzero anomalous dimension after fluctuations. Konishi is already long; this is not lifting of a perturbatively protected BPS class.

For a proposed 1/16-BPS lifting, zero-mode counting must be applied to the actual supercharge/mixing matrix element, with its partner insertion, corrected operator profiles, and fluctuations. No universal vanishing theorem for that calculation was established by the sources checked here.

## 5. The exact checked Konishi coefficient

Keep Alday–Korchemsky's Yang–Mills coupling g and vacuum angle theta:

$$
\tau=\frac{\theta}{2\pi}+\frac{4\pi i}{g^2},
\qquad e^{2\pi i\tau}=e^{-8\pi^2/g^2+i\theta}.
$$

Their gamma_K is the Konishi anomalous dimension; the following equality selects the leading one-instanton and anti-instanton term, not the complete answer:

$$
\left.\gamma_K\right|_{\text{leading one-instanton and anti-instanton term}}
=-\frac{27\kappa_N}{5(N^2-1)}
\left(\frac{g^2}{4\pi^2}\right)^2
\left(e^{2\pi i\tau}+e^{-2\pi i\bar\tau}\right),
$$

where their collective-coordinate factor is

$$
\kappa_N=\frac{2\Gamma(N-\tfrac12)}{\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma(N-1)}.
$$

Here Gamma is Euler's gamma function. Direct SU(2) check:

$$
\kappa_2=
\frac{2\Gamma(\tfrac32)}{\sqrt\pi\Gamma(1)}
=\frac{2(\sqrt\pi/2)}{\sqrt\pi}=1,
\qquad
-\frac{27\kappa_2}{5(2^2-1)}=-\frac95.
$$

This agrees with their Eq. (31). The SU(N) expression is Eq. (46); its separate OPE-coefficient formula was not used.

## 6. Generic finite-coupling constancy does not decide the weak cusp

For every nonnegative integer k,

$$
\log\left(g^{-k}e^{-8\pi^2/g^2}\right)
=-k\log g-\frac{8\pi^2}{g^2}\longrightarrow-\infty,
$$

$$
\lim_{g\to0^+}g^{-k}e^{-8\pi^2/g^2}=0.
$$

Thus a function can vanish at every perturbative order at g=0 while being nonzero at every finite positive g. The weak-coupling point is a cusp, not an ordinary interior point of the conformal manifold. Local constancy at generic finite coupling does not rule out a pairing present everywhere at finite weak coupling and invisible in the formal cusp expansion. This is a logical possibility for unprotected sectors, not an established N=4 example. Section 1 separately rules it out in the specified scalar sector.

## 7. A further unitarity constraint on a leading instanton effect

This is a new deduction, not a cited literature theorem. Restrict to a finite-dimensional fixed-charge space of complete perturbative BPS survivors, with the positive inner product inherited from radial quantization. Let H denote the nonnegative supersymmetry Hamiltonian on this space. Suppose its asymptotic leading nonperturbative matrix contains only instanton numbers +1 and −1, with no neutral contribution at the same exponential order. In this work proof, A denotes the coefficient matrix of instanton number +1 and the dagger is its adjoint in that positive inner product:

$$
H_{\mathrm{lead}}(\theta)
=e^{-8\pi^2/g^2}
\left(e^{i\theta}A+e^{-i\theta}A^\dagger\right).
$$

Positivity of the exact H for every theta implies positivity of its leading coefficient at every fixed theta; an exponentially smaller remainder cannot cure a strictly negative leading eigenvalue. Its angle average is

$$
\int_0^{2\pi} e^{i\theta}\,d\theta
=\int_0^{2\pi} e^{-i\theta}\,d\theta=0.
$$

$$
\int_0^{2\pi}H_{\mathrm{lead}}(\theta)\,d\theta=0,
\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}H_{\mathrm{lead}}(\theta)\ge0,
\qquad
\int_0^{2\pi}\operatorname{Tr}H_{\mathrm{lead}}(\theta)\,d\theta=0.
$$

A continuous nonnegative function of zero integral vanishes identically. A positive semidefinite matrix of zero trace is zero. Therefore

$$
H_{\mathrm{lead}}(\theta)=0\quad\text{for every }\theta,
\qquad A=0.
$$

This matrix argument includes all degenerate mixing and is invariant under unitary changes of basis; it does not select an eigenvalue branch before mixing.

This does not prohibit a supercharge pairing at one-instanton order. For the transferred effective supercharge on the perturbative survivor space, let d denote its leading instanton coefficient. If its first term is e^(2 pi i tau)d and the omitted terms are of higher exponential order, then

$$
\left.Q_{\mathrm{eff}}\right|_{\mathrm{lead}}=e^{2\pi i\tau}d,
\qquad
\left.\{Q_{\mathrm{eff}},Q_{\mathrm{eff}}^\dagger\}\right|_{\mathrm{lead}}
=e^{-16\pi^2/g^2}\{d,d^\dagger\}\ge0,
$$

since

$$
e^{2\pi i\tau}e^{-2\pi i\bar\tau}
=e^{-16\pi^2/g^2}.
$$

Thus a vanishing leading one-instanton anomalous dimension does not prove that the nonperturbative supercharge differential vanishes. The inference assumes the stated leading-sector expansion; it is not a general theorem excluding all nonperturbative effects.

## 8. Current cohomology literature and remaining target

- [2511.09519](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.09519): the SO(7)/USp(6) mismatch is resolved by a perturbative Konishi differential.
- [2610.22332v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.22332): its explicit fortuitous/monotone pairing is perturbative. Its discussion of instantons concerns the older proof of classical gaugino-ring nonvanishing, not an instanton-only lifting.
- [2610.20340v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.20340): SU(3) brane-fused towers are protected at first quantum order. This is not an all-loop or instanton theorem.

No examined primary source gives an actual instanton-only lifting of an N=4 class already certified to survive the complete perturbative differential. This is the scope of the checked literature, not an exhaustive absence theorem.

The meaningful unresolved target is outside the pure-scalar ring: first establish complete perturbative survival and an allowed partner, then calculate a nonperturbative differential or mixing matrix element. A corrected correlator, a corrected long-multiplet dimension, or an index comparison alone does not supply that result.
